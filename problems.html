Real Threat Analysis

The Modus Operandi: How Scammers Deceive You

Detailed breakdown of the most dangerous online frauds plaguing citizens today, along with key red flags to recognize them instantly.

Critical Alert - High Coercion

1. 'Digital Arrest' & Fake Law Enforcement Calls

Fraudsters pose as CBI, ED, Police, or Customs officers via WhatsApp or Skype video calls. They claim an illegal parcel containing narcotics or a fraudulent bank account is linked to your Aadhaar card.

Modus Operandi:

They display fake police station backdrops, wear uniforms, show forged arrest warrants, and order you to stay on video in a closed room until you transfer funds to a 'government verification account'.

Red Flags to Spot:

  • Indian law has NO provision for 'Digital Arrest' via video call.
  • Real police will NEVER demand money to verify innocence.
  • Extreme urgency, isolation demands, and threats of immediate jail.
Financial Scam - Daily Threat

2. UPI 'Pay to Receive' & QR Code Traps

You list an item on OLX or receive a call saying 'We are sending your lottery/refund'. The scammer sends a QR code or payment request link asking you to enter your UPI PIN.

The Golden Rule of UPI:

Entering your UPI PIN ALWAYS deducts money from your account. You NEVER need to enter a PIN to receive money.

Red Flags to Spot:

  • Any request to enter your secret PIN or scan a QR code to 'receive funds'.
  • Over-eager buyers who pay before seeing the item and insist only on UPI.
  • Screenshot of fake payment SMS that does not reflect in your actual bank app balance.
Malware & Phishing Alert

3. Fake Electricity Bill / KYC Suspension APKs

Victims receive SMS or WhatsApp messages claiming: 'Dear consumer, your electricity will be disconnected tonight at 9:30 PM due to unpaid bill. Call 98765xxxxx immediately.'

Modus Operandi:

When called, they ask you to pay a ₹10 recharge via a malicious link or download an APK file (e.g., 'BijliBill.apk' or 'QuickSupport'). This gives them complete remote control of your phone and OTPs.

Red Flags to Spot:

  • Sent from personal mobile numbers instead of official sender IDs (e.g., VM-MSEDCL).
  • Asking you to download `.apk` files or remote screen-sharing tools.
  • Unrealistic deadlines designed to trigger immediate panic.
Youth & Student Trap

4. Telegram 'Part-Time Job' & YouTube Like Scams

Scammers promise ₹3,000–₹5,000 daily for liking YouTube videos or writing Google reviews. Initially, they pay small sums (₹150–₹500) to build trust.

The Big Trap:

Once hooked, victims are moved to Telegram VIP groups and coerced to invest in 'prepaid task portfolios' or crypto investments with fake dashboards showing huge profits. When victims try to withdraw, scammers demand further 'clearance fees' until savings are wiped out.

Red Flags to Spot:

  • Offers of huge pay for trivial tasks like liking video links.
  • Being directed to private Telegram or WhatsApp groups.
  • Demanding money in advance to unlock earned earnings or commissions.
